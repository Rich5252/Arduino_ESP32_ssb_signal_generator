Predistort LUT vs. a linear offset/scale window — the 'D' on/off comparison

Same 1024-point 'E' sweep methodology, two passes: 'D' ON (the full nonlinear predistort LUT) vs. 'D' OFF with pwm_offset/scale set to 28%/54% — a window chosen from the REVISION 6 raw duty sweep specifically because duty→amplitude looked closest to a straight line there. This is the comparison the original two-pass plan was for: does a well-chosen simple linear mapping get close to what the LUT does?

The linear window can't reach a real "off" state. Picking offset/scale to land inside the straightest part of the real duty→amplitude curve means starting well past the dead zone by construction — so at commanded frac=0 the linear mapping still outputs duty 286, sitting at −59.08 dBm instead of the LUT's −98.43 dBm. That's a genuine, structural 39.4 dB worse floor, not a measurement artifact — no single linear offset/scale choice can be both linear in-range AND reach near-zero output, which is exactly the problem the nonlinear LUT exists to solve.

'D' off residual from its own best-fit line

Fit amplitude = m·frac + c (a straight line, not a proportional-through-origin curve, since this mapping was never meant to reach zero) to the 'D'-off run, then plot the gap. Near-zero and flat across most of the range would mean the chosen window really is close to linear; the visible tilt at the very low end shows where the window predicted from the REVISION 6 data doesn't quite hold on this real run.

In-range, the well-chosen linear window comes surprisingly close to the LUT. 0.21dB RMS / 1.34dB max against its own best-fit line is only modestly worse than the LUT's 0.14dB RMS / 0.55dB max against a true proportional target — and over frac 0.5–1.0 alone, fitting the SAME proportional model used for 'D' on gives 0.21dB RMS / 0.49dB max, essentially matching the LUT. This confirms the offset/scale window picked from the REVISION 6 duty sweep (see the earlier analysis) was a good call for linearity — the real cost of skipping the LUT shows up entirely as the floor gap above, not as extra ripple in the working range.

The largest single deviations from the straight-line fit (frac ≈ 0.18, 0.46, 0.62, 0.92 — visible as brief spikes in the residual trace) look like isolated measurement glitches (a single point recovering the next step) rather than a systematic trend, similar in character to the handful of one-off dips seen in the 'D'-on run.
