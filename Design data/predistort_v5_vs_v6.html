V5 active range

Dead zone: duty 1–9 (<1% of range), then normalized output crosses 1% of full swing by duty~56.
Productive climb: duty 9→653 — 63% of the full duty range. Near-saturation (within 0.3dB of max): duty 653–1023, 36% of the range, collapsed into the last table bin.

V6 active range

Dead zone: duty 1 only (exact-tie pooling), but normalized output doesn't cross 1% of full swing until duty~166 — a longer practical dead zone than V5's, despite the smaller pooled block.
Productive climb: duty 2→916 — 89% of the full duty range. Near-saturation (within 0.3dB of max): only duty 870–1023, 15% of the range.

Two confirmed causes, stacked. RX attenuation dropped 10dB for this sweep (30dB pad vs. V5's 40dB, for better low-end sensitivity) — that alone explains most of the raw ceiling shift (measured 11.1dB, close to the expected 10dB). Separately, the RSET/PWM gate-drive range was deliberately redesigned (MOSFET gate now 0.94V at duty=0 up to 3.38V at duty=1023, engineered to not compromise BJT bias or filter/group-delay stability) — a real reduction in gain per duty count, built to use more of the 1024-count range.

Shape, not just position, genuinely changed. Correcting V6's readings by the 11.1dB ceiling-anchored offset and comparing duty-for-duty against V5 shows a large, duty-dependent residual that attenuation alone can't produce: ~27.9dB less real output at duty=100, narrowing to under 0.2dB by duty=900. That's the gate-drive redesign working as intended. It confirms the active-range improvement above is real, not a normalization artifact of the wider floor-to-ceiling span: 89% of V6's duty range is doing useful work (vs. V5's 63%), and the near-saturation collapse that ate over a third of V5's range shrinks to about a seventh of V6's.
