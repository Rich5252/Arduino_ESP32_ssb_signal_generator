# Project status, 2026-09-06 — what's settled, what's open, what's proposed but not built

A topic-organized read of `group_delay_fit_notes.md` and `ssb_mic_test_commands.md`, pulled together on request rather than added as another dated log entry. The two source files remain the authoritative chronological record; this is a snapshot view across them, organized by subsystem instead of by date, so it will go stale as new entries land there — treat it as a one-time "where are we" rather than a third document to keep in sync.

## The big picture

The project has been through a long, disciplined loop on the envelope path's group-delay equalizer (`gdeq`): fit a coefficient pair against increasingly better data (LTspice → reconstructed real curves → real HiRes TFA sweeps), validate it on real hardware, then check whether flattening group delay actually improves two-tone IMD. That last step has now failed to deliver a clean win **twice** with two different, well-validated coefficient sets (the a+A candidate and candidate B) — both flatten the group-delay curve exactly as predicted on real hardware, and neither produces an unambiguous IMD improvement. That's the load-bearing finding sitting under everything else right now: this project's own additive group-delay model is accurate to a few microseconds RMS against real measurements, but group delay alone isn't the thing standing between here and better IMD. All three coefficient sets (default, a+A, candidate B) are live-selectable via `'G'`, none is recommended as the new default, and the honest current position is documented as "not a resolution — the finer question of what's really controlling real-world IMD is still open."

Two other subsystems reached real, adopted conclusions: `envelope_ampeq` shelf 1 (`'a'`) is a confirmed, recommended-on improvement (real IMD benefit, magnitude tracks prediction), while shelf 2 (`'A'`) was tried, measured, and reverted — real magnitude recovery but roughly double the delay dispersion and marginally worse IMD, so the recommendation is shelf-1-only. And the `eq` mechanism question (why does the audio-path EQ have the single biggest positive IMD effect of anything tried) got fully answered: it's the presence peak's amplitude mismatch between the two tones keeping the envelope off a true zero null, not the highpass filter's phase shift — confirmed both by elimination and by directly reproducing the effect with a deliberate amplitude offset (`'R'`).

Worth flagging up front, though, since it doesn't show up as a dated entry anywhere in either file: `envelope_gdeq.h`'s own header comment has called the unexplained AM-to-PM crosstalk asymmetry (`'h'` AM-isolation test: -2.4kHz sideband nulls out cleanly, +2.4kHz sits stuck at -40dB) "the current top-priority open item" since early in the project, and recommended running that exact `'h'` A/B with `'g'` on vs off as a direct check. Neither file shows that check ever being run. Given that the leading unexplained mechanism behind the repeated "flat group delay, mixed IMD result" finding is explicitly called "AM-to-PM or nonlinearity-driven" (2026-09-06 candidate-B writeup), this old, still-open, never-actually-tested item may be more central to the current mystery than its age in the backlog suggests.

## Group-delay equalizer (gdeq) and its candidates

Current state: three coefficient sets exist and cycle via `'G'` (default, a+A candidate, candidate B), all fitted for the PNP_BC327_ATTN filter at 16000Hz specifically, all group-delay-validated on real hardware to within a few microseconds RMS of their model predictions. `env_gdeq_enable` (`'g'`) itself is off at compile time and in every preset that doesn't explicitly turn it on; where it is on, the default coefficient pair is what's active unless `'G'` has been cycled live (and no preset currently persists a non-default `'G'` choice — see the note under Presets below).

Open: candidate B's real IMD gain is small and depends on landing `relative_delay` inside a narrow 2.00–2.10 sample "compromise window" where different IMD orders trade off against each other rather than sharing one clean optimum — the fine-step delay command (`';'`/`'''`, 0.01 samples) exists now specifically to explore that window, but the actual best point in it hasn't been reported yet. The a+A candidate's own 2026-09-04 "no measurable IMD difference vs default" result was later flagged as potentially unreliable, since it predates the discovery that the gdeq/ampeq processing chain was silently running at 4x its intended rate during `'w'` chirp captures — that specific IMD comparison is now marked "unconfirmed rather than settled" in the notes, not re-run. A parallel design-search question — whether a completely different cascaded-allpass architecture (3 or 4 sections instead of 2) could do better — was explored only in passing (diminishing returns noted, "worth revisiting if 14.7us turns out to matter") and never followed up.

Proposed but not built: a direct head-to-head IMD3/IMD5 spectrum comparison between the two supported analog filter variants (BC337 vs. PNP_BC327_ATTN), each with its own properly-retuned delay, to settle whether the PNP+attenuator filter's extra insertion loss actually costs anything in practice — flagged 2026-09-01, never run (only the PNP+attn filter has been pursued since). Also proposed and not built: firmware auto-selecting the chirp sweep's top frequency based on which gdeq/ampeq state is active, instead of the current manual `CHIRP_F1_HZ` edit.

## Envelope amplitude EQ (ampeq shelves)

Shelf 1 (`'a'`) is validated and recommended on — real IMD benefit, magnitude tracks its model to a few tenths of a dB, and a real mechanism confirmed on the bench (it "gates" the envelope null transient faster and deeper). Shelf 2 (`'A'`) was built, measured, and the recommendation settled on shelf-1-only: real high-frequency magnitude recovery but roughly double the delay dispersion (75.7→157.5µs) and marginally worse IMD when combined with shelf 1. Both flags are independently toggleable and both default to off at compile time; worth noting that despite shelf 1 being the actual bench recommendation, none of the 10 presets in `settings.h` currently turn it on — the recommendation lives only as a live `'a'` toggle, not as a persisted preset choice, the same gap noted for gdeq variant selection below.

## The `eq` mechanism and envelope floor/predistortion

Fully resolved: `eq`'s IMD benefit comes from the presence peak's amplitude mismatch between the two test tones, not the highpass filter's phase shift — confirmed by elimination and then directly reproduced with `'R'`'s deliberate tone-ratio offset. One loose end from that investigation was never built, though: a proposal to split `eq_enable` into two independently-toggleable flags (HPF vs. presence) so the two mechanisms could be A/B'd directly on the bench, rather than inferred — parked pending go-ahead, never implemented (the question got answered a different way, but the split-flag feature itself doesn't exist). A related, more ambitious proposal — a dedicated swept-sine TFA-style test mode for `eq` itself, since it runs upstream of the Hilbert transform where the existing chirp fast-path doesn't reach — was also scoped and parked, not built.

Separately: `'x'` (envelope floor) was bench-confirmed to make IMDs worse in every case tried and isn't recommended. Envelope pre-distortion (`'D'`) has real measured-curve backing but is explicitly still "not validated beyond the measurement itself" — no bench IMD test has been run with it on.

## Envelope output interpolation (`'I'`)

Settled as off by default after real-hardware listening found no interpolation setting free of an audible "burble," and the mechanism was narrowed to float-ramp/wake-rate cost rather than analog/ADC coupling (the earlier hypothesis was retracted). Two pieces of that mechanism finding are flagged in the notes themselves as provisional rather than fully confirmed: which curve state (HOLD vs. Catmull-Rom) was actually active during part of the wake-rate-cost testing, and whether the wake-rate-alone cost is fully separable from the float-ramp cost. Two direct follow-up measurements were proposed to nail this down — a controlled GPIO2 scope comparison and a timing probe directly around the interpolation curve branches — and neither has been run.

## `freq_dev` slew-rate limiter

Validated on real hardware: tightening the limit below 8000Hz/sample worsens IMD, loosening toward 12000–20000Hz gains 1-2dB, and the firmware's own assumed worst-case swing (~8000Hz) was shown to be an underestimate (true worst case ~9640Hz at a sign crossing). Not yet done: raising `FREQ_DEV_SLEW_MAX_FINITE_HZ` so the interactive `'{'`/`'}'` step can actually reach that empirically better 12000–20000Hz range (it's currently capped below it), correcting the stale code comment that goes with it, and a finer sweep across 8000–24000Hz-plus (including fully unlimited) to pin down a real optimum rather than the two or three points already tried.

## Presets and persistence

The `'0'`–`'9'` preset system and the `'P'` dump-to-pasteable-line command both correctly cover every current lever, including the 3-way gdeq variant field added this week — confirmed working, not a gap. The gap that does exist: because every trailing `PersistentSettings` field (ampeq shelf 1/2, gdeq variant) was added after the 10 existing presets were written, all 10 rely on C's zero-fill and so all load with ampeq off and gdeq on default, regardless of what the bench testing above actually recommends. Nothing wrong with that — it's a deliberate "don't silently change an existing preset's behavior" choice — but it does mean the shelf-1-on / gdeq-variant-chosen recommendations from the last several weeks of testing aren't reachable via a single preset digit today; getting there means dialing them in live (`'a'`, `'G'`) after loading a preset, or pasting a new preset line via `'P'` once a live combination is settled on.

## Hardware/GPIO odds and ends

A cluster of ADC-continuous-driver GPIO coupling bugs got root-caused and fixed this project (the GPIO13 5kHz pulse, the GPIO39-stuck-low addressing bug), but two adjacent, same-class suspects were flagged and never actually checked: whether the AD9851 bit-bang interface pins (GPIO9-12) suffer the same class of coupling, and whether GPIO39 itself has been independently re-verified clean now that it's in active use (both explicitly "flagged, not yet tested" rather than ruled out).

## Smaller unresolved threads worth knowing about

`MAX_FREQ_DEV_HZ` (currently 8000Hz) is explicitly not a landed permanent value, just "kept for now." Whether the PNP+attenuator filter's extra high-frequency insertion loss costs anything in real IMD was flagged in the same September 1st entry as the filter comparison above and never measured. A single unexplained spectral outlier ("low 10k leakage") from one measurement session was left flagged pending a repeat rather than chased down. And the minimum tone-amplitude mismatch actually needed to keep the envelope off a true null — confirmed to exist and to work, via `'R'` — was never mapped to a minimum value or checked across the other `'T'` band-preset pairs beyond the original 700/1900Hz pair.
