Shelf2 on real hardware: more magnitude, more than double the dispersion

Two real TFA sweeps ('g'+'a' both on): ga_Trial1_TF.txt (shelf1 only, 2500Hz/+6dB) vs. ga_Trial2_TF.txt (shelf1+shelf2, adding 6000Hz/+10dB). Same board, same method, only the ampeq firmware differs. User's real IMD report with shelf2 on: marginally worse than shelf1-only.

The predicted trade-off happened, close to as predicted

Magnitude at 8000Hz improved by a real +6.7dB (net -16.0dB → -9.3dB — short of the +10dB analytic prediction, so shelf2 recovered somewhat less than modeled, but still a large, genuine improvement). Group-delay dispersion over 100–8000Hz roughly doubled (75.7 µs p-p → 157.5 µs p-p), with the largest single change right at 8000Hz (+85 µs, vs. shelf2's own +62 µs analytic prediction at that point — real hardware shows somewhat more top-end delay growth than the idealized model). Mean delay barely moved either time (197.1 → 196.4 µs), consistent with both shelves' near-zero mean delay contribution. This is a direct, real confirmation of the 2026-09-04 prediction: more high-frequency gain recovery costs more nearby delay distortion, and gdeq was never refit to absorb it.

Reading this alongside the IMD report

The user's real two-tone IMD result with shelf2 on was marginally worse than shelf1-only — not dramatically worse, despite dispersion roughly doubling. Read together with the magnitude gain, that's informative on its own: the ~6.7dB of extra high-frequency amplitude correction and the ~82 µs of extra p-p dispersion appear to be landing close to a wash for two-tone IMD in this unrefit state — not a clean win for either effect alone. That's consistent with (not proof of) this project's working model that BOTH amplitude symmetry and delay flatness matter for IMD, rather than one dominating — and it's exactly why a fair test of "does more amplitude correction help" needs gdeq refit first, so the delay side isn't moving at the same time.

Decision as of 2026-09-04: revert to shelf1-only (better real compromise as currently unrefit) while a wider IMD comparison is recorded. Shelf2 stays in the firmware (still selected via the same 'a' flag, both stages cascade together) so it's available to re-test later, ideally after a gdeq refit against the combined analog+shelf1+shelf2 phase response — see group_delay_fit_notes.md's matching entries.
