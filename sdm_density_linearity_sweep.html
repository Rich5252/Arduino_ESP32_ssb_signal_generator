Floor and ceiling match to within ~0.3–0.5dB — strong confirmation both paths bottom out on and saturate against the same physical chain. But the shape isn't identical: SDM's real signal emerges noticeably earlier in its own command range (dead-zone-to-turn-on around 16% of range vs. PWM's ~29%), then SDM compresses slightly more at the very top (last 15% vs. 11% squashed within 0.3dB of max). Both curves show a similar rate of small (<0.3dB, one exception at 0.32dB) non-monotonic single-step wiggles through their climb — consistent with ordinary spectrum-analyzer read noise on this bench, not a defect specific to either modulation scheme.

Works, but measurably worse than PWM's own native fit. The unmodified PWM LUT already gets SDM into the right ballpark — no gross mistracking, no runaway error — but RMS residual is roughly 1.5–1.8× and max residual roughly 2× PWM's own 'E'-validation benchmark (0.134dB RMS / 0.545dB max over the same frac ranges). The worst points cluster in the steep turn-on region (frac 0.03–0.14), exactly where the raw-sweep comparison above shows SDM and PWM's curves diverge most in shape. Reusing the PWM table is a reasonable stopgap — it is not obviously broken — but a dedicated SDM-density predistort table (same methodology as REVISION 4–6: exhaustive density=−127..127 direct sweep, isotonic-smoothed, PCHIP-inverted) would likely close most of this gap, the same way REVISION 4 closed PWM's own gate-voltage-inference gap.
